# Job evaluation: this kit against the EU guidelines

In March 2026 the European Commission and the European Institute for Gender Equality published
*EU-wide guidelines on gender-neutral job evaluation and classification: Step-by-step toolkit*
([doi:10.2839/7400453](https://eige.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/gender-neutral-job-evaluation-and-classification-step-by-step-toolkit.pdf),
CC BY 4.0). They are voluntary: using them "does not certify or guarantee compliance", and the
Italian decree names EIGE's tools among those its monitoring body can draw on (D.lgs. 96/2026,
art. 14(1)(b)). This note sets the kit's grid against them, says where the kit takes a shortcut,
and what a real engagement would do instead.

## Which pathway applies

The guidelines offer three pathways by size: a graduated factor comparison for micro-organisations
(fewer than 10 workers), a pair comparison for small and medium-sized ones, and a standard
point-factor method for organisations with more than 250 workers or with diverse, structured roles.
Every employer in this kit has more than 250 workers, so the **standard point-factor method** is
the benchmark.

## Side by side

| | EU guidelines, standard pathway | This kit |
|---|---|---|
| Factors | Skills, responsibility, effort, working conditions | The same four (Article 4(4)) |
| Subfactors | 14: knowledge; interpersonal and communication; problem-solving; planning and organisation; physical skills; responsibility for people, goods and equipment, information, financial resources; mental, psychosocial and emotional, physical effort; physical and psychological environment, organisational environment | None: one score per factor |
| Levels | 0 (not required) to 5 (highly complex or strategic), per subfactor | 1 to 5, per factor |
| Weights | 40 / 35 / 15 / 10, split across subfactors; given as illustrative, to be adapted for an objective, gender-neutral reason | 35 / 35 / 15 / 15 |
| Points | Up to 1,200 | 100 to 500 |
| Who scores | A job evaluation committee: each member scores alone from the job profile, then the committee agrees one level per subfactor; workers' or union representatives involved | One analyst, with a written rationale per role, for a dry run |
| Bias checks | Test the weights on women- and men-dominated jobs; watch for halo effects; rate the job, not the job holder; do not drop subfactors common in women-dominated jobs | Two explicit choices: customer service scores high on effort for sustained emotional load; HR scores extra responsibility for confidential data and legal compliance |

## What the shortcut costs

The kit scores four factors, not fourteen subfactors. That is enough to show the method and to
build categories that cross departments, but it hides the subfactors the guidelines single out
because they are most often undervalued in jobs held mostly by women: interpersonal and
communication skills, responsibility for people, and psychosocial and emotional effort. In the
kit they are folded into a single factor score, and only the rationale column shows whether they
were counted.

The weights matter as much as the scores. Section 4 of the [readiness report](../reports/readiness_report.md)
reruns the categories and the Article 10 screen with the guidelines' illustrative weights: a few
roles at the edge of a point band change category, and the list of flagged categories and the
cost ceiling change with them. The weights are a negotiated choice, and the guidelines ask for it
to be made transparently, tested and documented before anyone looks at pay.

## What a real engagement would do

1. **Start from the collective agreement in Italy.** Its levels are the reference for work of equal
   value and are presumed compliant (D.lgs. 96/2026, art. 4(1)-(4)); job evaluation refines them.
2. **Score the fourteen subfactors**, with the guidelines' Excel worksheet, through a committee that
   includes workers' representatives.
3. **Agree the weights first**, test them on women- and men-dominated roles, and record why they
   differ from the illustrative ones, if they do.
4. **Map the subfactor scores into this kit.** Its point bands and every later step stay as they
   are: replace `data/job_evaluation.csv` with one row per role and its total points, and rerun.
